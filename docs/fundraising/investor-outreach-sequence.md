# Investor Outreach Sequence

## Best Starting Path

Start with:

1. friendly angels and operators
2. angels with SaaS, CRM, operations, or distribution background
3. selected micro-VCs interested in vertical AI or workflow software
4. only later, larger institutional firms

## 4-Step Sequence

### Step 1: Warm Mapping

Build the first list from:

- founders you know
- operators in distribution and wholesale
- angels who like workflow software
- investors already talking about vertical AI, systems of action, self-hosted infra, or emerging-market B2B

Do not start with random cold blasts.

### Step 2: Short Intro Ask

Best initial ask:

`I’m not running a broad process yet. I’m pressure-testing a narrow wedge and looking for a few smart early conversations.`

That lowers pressure and gets more responses.

### Step 3: 15-Minute Meeting

Structure:

1. painful workflow
2. why incumbents fail here
3. short demo
4. wedge and GTM
5. what proof you are building next

### Step 4: Follow-Up

Send:

- the one-pager
- one screenshot or short Loom
- design-partner progress
- one clear next milestone

## Cold Investor Email

Subject:

`Vertical AI workflow wedge in WhatsApp-first distribution`

Body:

`Hi [Name],`

`I’m building an AI operating CRM for distributors and wholesalers in WhatsApp-first markets.`

`The product starts with one workflow: inbound inquiry -> quote/proforma draft -> follow-up -> deal progression -> collections follow-through.`

`The thesis is that legacy CRMs are systems of record, but this category needs a controlled system of action with self-hosting and auditability built in.`

`I’m at the design-partner stage and speaking with a small number of angels and micro-VCs who understand vertical AI and workflow software.`

`If that is in your strike zone, I’d love to send a one-pager or show a short demo.`

`Best,`

`[Your Name]`

## Follow-Up Email After First Meeting

Subject:

`Thanks + next milestone`

Body:

`Thanks again for the time.`

`The key thing we are testing now is whether distributors and wholesalers will buy this wedge even if they keep their current CRM.`

`Over the next [X] weeks we are focused on signing [3-5] design partners and measuring response-time, quote-turnaround, and follow-up ROI.`

`Attached is the one-pager. Happy to keep you updated as the proof package develops.`

## What To Avoid

- sending a huge deck cold
- saying you are replacing Salesforce
- leading with feature inventory
- overclaiming autonomy
- pitching too many customer segments at once

## What Good Investor Interest Looks Like

- they quickly understand the wedge
- they ask about proof and ROI, not just features
- they introduce design partners or relevant operators
- they want updates after the next milestone, even if they do not invest immediately
