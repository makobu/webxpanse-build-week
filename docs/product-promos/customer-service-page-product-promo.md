# Customer Service Page

Client-facing product promo and feature summary

Prepared: June 24, 2026

## Product Positioning

The Customer Service page is the post-purchase care center inside WebXpanse and Clarity CRM. In the product, this surface is labeled Customer Care. It helps a business keep paying customers from going quiet after the sale by showing who needs a check-in, who needs attention, who has gone quiet, and what the next useful relationship step should be.

This page is not a simple support inbox. It is a customer relationship workbench. It begins after purchase evidence exists, such as a paid invoice, a closed-won deal, a workspace account purchase, or a manual customer care override. That distinction matters because the page is built for retention, trust, renewals, value delivery, and customer growth.

For a client, the value is simple: Customer Service helps turn a sale into a relationship the business continues to protect.

## Executive Summary

Many businesses work hard to win a customer and then lose momentum after the purchase. The invoice is paid. The deal is closed. The project begins. Then follow-up starts depending on memory, scattered notes, old messages, or whoever happens to remember the customer next.

The Customer Service page solves that problem by giving the team a dedicated place to manage paying customers after the sale. It organizes customers into practical views such as Today, Needs attention, Quiet, and All. It shows each customer's health score, purchase context, last touch, next touch, suggested next step, owner, and follow-up plan. It also provides quick actions to check in, create a task, schedule a check-in, mark a customer at risk, prepare renewal care, create an expansion path, pause care, or add customers to a follow-up plan.

The page is also designed for safe automation. It shows whether Customer Care automation is in suggest-only mode, auto-safe mode, needs more learning, or is paused. The system can suggest next steps and help create internal actions, but customer-facing messages still require human approval when policy calls for it.

The result is not "a customer service screen." The result is a repeatable customer care rhythm that helps the business protect revenue, reduce churn, and create more value after the first purchase.

## What The Customer Service Page Is

The Customer Service page is a post-purchase care workspace.

It uses customer records, purchase evidence, activity history, tasks, campaigns, health signals, cadence rules, and follow-up plans to help the team decide what care should happen next.

In practical terms, it gives the workspace:

- A focused list of paying customers.
- Tabs for today, needs attention, quiet customers, and all customers.
- Customer health score and status signals.
- Purchase context from paid invoices, closed-won deals, workspace purchases, or manual overrides.
- Suggested next steps for each customer.
- Quick actions for check-ins, tasks, scheduling, risk, renewal, expansion, pausing, and plans.
- Filters by owner, due date, status, follow-up plan, customer status, signal, rhythm, and stale check-ins.
- A follow-up plan drawer for reusable care rhythms.
- A customer detail page for deeper context.
- Automation status that explains whether the system can only suggest, needs more learning, is paused, or can safely assist internal care steps.

## What The Customer Service Page Is Not

The Customer Service page should be positioned honestly.

It is not a general help desk ticket system. It is not a public support portal. It is not built for random visitors to request help. It is not meant to replace human customer judgment. It does not automatically message customers without the right controls. It does not treat every lead as a customer.

It starts after purchase evidence.

That is why it matters. The page is built for customers the business has already earned and now needs to keep.

## Salient Features

### 1. Post-Purchase Focus

The page is designed around paying customers. Customer Care fills after purchase evidence such as a paid invoice, a closed-won deal, a workspace account purchase, or a manual customer care override.

Client value:

- The team focuses on real customers, not every lead.
- Customer care starts from a meaningful business event.
- The business can protect relationships after the sale.

### 2. Today View

The Today tab shows customers who need a check-in now. It helps users start with the most immediate care work.

Client value:

- The team can open the page and know what needs attention today.
- Follow-up becomes easier to prioritize.
- Important customer check-ins are less likely to be forgotten.

### 3. Needs Attention View

The Needs attention tab highlights customers who may be at risk, have low health, or need a touch.

Client value:

- Risk becomes easier to see before the customer leaves.
- The team can act earlier.
- Customer support becomes more proactive.

### 4. Quiet Customer View

The Quiet tab helps identify customers who have gone silent or have not been touched in too long.

Client value:

- Quiet customers are not ignored by accident.
- The business can restart useful conversations.
- Relationship risk becomes visible.

### 5. All Customer View

The All tab gives a broader customer care list for review, filtering, and planning.

Client value:

- Managers can see the full post-purchase care picture.
- Operators can audit coverage.
- Teams can avoid treating customer care as scattered one-off work.

### 6. Customer Health Score

Each profile includes a health score from 0 to 100. The score considers signals such as engagement, time since last touch, open tasks, campaigns, purchase or deal evidence, and at-risk status.

Client value:

- Customer risk is easier to understand.
- Teams can compare relationships more quickly.
- The page helps turn vague concern into visible care signals.

### 7. Why This Customer Needs Care

The page explains why a customer is shown, using plain labels such as Check-in due, Needs attention, Quiet customer, Renewal coming, Growth opportunity, Keep in touch, or Paused.

Client value:

- Users do not have to guess why the customer appears.
- The next action feels clearer.
- Less training is needed for everyday use.

### 8. Suggested Next Step

Each customer can show a suggested next step, such as confirming outcome progress, exploring value growth, reopening a quiet conversation, or reviewing whether care should continue.

Client value:

- Users have a practical starting point.
- Check-ins become more useful than generic "just following up" messages.
- The business can focus on customer value, not just contact frequency.

### 9. Purchase Context

The page shows purchase context such as the purchase title, entry source, purchase amount, and date when available.

Client value:

- Users remember what the customer bought.
- Follow-up can be tied to the promised outcome.
- The team avoids checking in without context.

### 10. Owner Visibility

Customer care profiles can show an owner. Filters also allow users to view customers by owner or find unassigned relationships.

Client value:

- Accountability is clearer.
- Managers can spot unowned customer relationships.
- Customers are less likely to fall between roles.

### 11. Follow-Up Rhythm

Customer care profiles support rhythms such as weekly, every two weeks, monthly, quarterly, and manual.

Client value:

- Different customers can receive the right level of attention.
- Hot or at-risk customers can be reviewed more often.
- Stable relationships do not need unnecessary noise.

### 12. Last Touch And Next Touch

The page shows the last customer touch and the next planned check-in.

Client value:

- Users can see whether the relationship is fresh or stale.
- Next actions become time-based instead of memory-based.
- Managers can review care coverage more easily.

### 13. Quick Check-In

Users can mark a customer check-in complete directly from the page.

Client value:

- Completed relationship work is captured.
- The next check-in can be recalculated from the customer's cadence.
- Customer care history becomes more reliable.

### 14. Quick Task Creation

Users can create a customer check-in task directly from the page. The task can carry the customer link, suggested description, due date, owner, priority, and customer care metadata.

Client value:

- Follow-up becomes assigned work.
- Customer care moves into the task system.
- Important next steps no longer live only in someone's head.

### 15. Schedule Check-In

Users can schedule the next customer check-in, commonly seven days ahead from the quick action.

Client value:

- The team can postpone care intentionally instead of forgetting it.
- The customer remains in the system's rhythm.
- Scheduling supports calmer daily execution.

### 16. Flag Risk

Selected customers can be marked at risk. This changes their lane and signals that the relationship needs review.

Client value:

- Users can quickly surface concern.
- Risk does not stay hidden in private judgment.
- The business can respond before churn or disappointment grows.

### 17. Prepare Renewal

Selected customers can be queued for renewal care. The page can schedule future attention and set a reason focused on confirming continued value.

Client value:

- Renewal conversations start earlier.
- The team can prepare based on customer value.
- Retention becomes part of the operating rhythm.

### 18. Create Expansion Path

Selected customers can be moved into a value-growth or expansion lane when there is a signal that more value could be created.

Client value:

- Customer success can lead to more revenue.
- Growth conversations become intentional.
- The team can ask what has changed since purchase and where help is needed next.

### 19. Pause Care

Users can pause a customer care profile when active follow-up should stop temporarily.

Client value:

- The team can control the care rhythm.
- Paused relationships are explicit instead of forgotten.
- The page respects real operating decisions.

### 20. Follow-Up Plans Drawer

The page includes a Follow-up Plans drawer. Users can add selected customers to existing plans or create simple plans without leaving the main page.

Client value:

- Repeated care rhythms are easier to manage.
- Several customers can follow the same plan.
- The team avoids rebuilding the same check-in process again and again.

### 21. Plan Presets

The page includes useful plan presets such as First 30 Days, Quiet Customer, Renewal, and Quarterly Check-in.

Client value:

- The business can start with practical care patterns.
- New users do not need to invent a system from scratch.
- The page teaches what good customer care rhythms look like.

### 22. Advanced Follow-Up Plans

The advanced plans page supports reusable check-in plans for paying customers. Plans can have a type, cadence, purpose, and optional campaign link.

Client value:

- Customer care can become a repeatable program.
- Teams can manage active plans and enrollment counts.
- Campaigns and care rhythms can stay connected when needed.

### 23. Bulk Actions

Users can select multiple customers and apply actions such as creating tasks, scheduling check-ins, marking check-ins complete, flagging risk, preparing renewal care, creating expansion paths, pausing care, or enrolling in plans.

Client value:

- Teams can manage care at scale.
- Managers can clean up a customer queue quickly.
- Repetitive work becomes less manual.

### 24. Practical Filters

The page includes filters for owner, due date, status, follow-up plan, customer status, signal, follow-up rhythm, and stale check-ins.

Client value:

- Users can find the right customer group quickly.
- Managers can review specific care risks.
- Operators can focus on the work that matters today.

### 25. Responsive Table And Card Views

The desktop view uses a table for scanning many customers. The mobile view uses customer cards for smaller screens.

Client value:

- The page works for office users and mobile operators.
- Customer care can be reviewed from different devices.
- Important actions remain accessible on smaller screens.

### 26. Client-Friendly Empty State

If there are no paying customers in Customer Care yet, the page explains why and points users toward deals, invoices, and contacts.

Client value:

- Empty space becomes guidance.
- Users understand how customers enter the care system.
- Setup confusion is reduced.

### 27. Customer Detail Page

Opening a customer shows a deeper care record. The detail page includes why the customer is here, suggested next step, purchase context, follow-up plan, touchpoints, open tasks, and customer care profile controls.

Client value:

- Users can move from queue scanning to detailed action.
- The customer story becomes easier to understand.
- Follow-up decisions have better context.

### 28. Touchpoint History

The system can record touchpoints such as planned work, manual tasks, campaigns, activities, check-ins, renewal touches, expansion touches, and risk recovery.

Client value:

- Customer care has history.
- Teams can review what happened before.
- The business can prove that care work is being done.

### 29. Open Task Awareness

The detail page can show open tasks for the customer.

Client value:

- Users avoid creating duplicate follow-up.
- Current commitments stay visible.
- Customer service becomes connected to execution.

### 30. Marketing Handoff Context

When the user has marketing access, the customer detail page can show marketing handoff lineage from campaigns, landing pages, conversion goals, and related tasks.

Client value:

- Post-sale care can understand where the customer came from.
- Marketing promises and customer outcomes can stay connected.
- The team sees more of the customer journey.

### 31. Automation Status

The page shows Customer Care automation status. It can explain whether automation is suggest-only, auto-safe, needs more learning, or paused.

Client value:

- The business knows how much control the system currently has.
- Users understand why automation may be limited.
- Trust grows because automation state is visible.

### 32. Human-Controlled Automation

Customer Care automation can suggest check-ins, create internal tasks, schedule check-ins, enroll follow-up plans, record check-ins, and draft customer replies under governance controls.

Client value:

- Routine internal care work can be assisted.
- Customer-facing drafts still require the right review.
- The system learns from accepted, edited, completed, or failed outcomes.

### 33. Learning Review Link

When automation is limited, the page can show blockers and link users to review learning.

Client value:

- The business can understand what automation still needs.
- Operators can improve the system over time.
- Automation becomes earned, not assumed.

### 34. Permission-Aware Actions

The page checks permissions for reading Customer Care, updating records, managing plans, and creating tasks.

Client value:

- Sensitive customer care work stays controlled.
- Not every user can change every care record.
- Teams can separate visibility from management authority.

### 35. Page Guide Support

When available, the page includes a guide button for Customer Care.

Client value:

- Users can learn the page without a separate training session.
- Adoption is easier for new teams.
- The product explains the workflow where the work happens.
